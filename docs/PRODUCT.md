# The product, without the fog

## The actual problem

“I need a deck” can mean at least four different things:

1. This page is overloaded.
2. This Film & TV project needs a structure.
3. The deck is not the first useful move.
4. I do not understand the language people are using.

Treating those as one intake form produces the interface equivalent of airport security: long, anxious, and convinced every detail is equally urgent. Here’s The Thing keeps the problems separate, then lets evidence travel only when it genuinely saves work.

## The four front doors

### Slide Check

Cross-industry. Paste only the words people must read. Choose whether the slide is presented live, sent to read, or forced to do both. The tool measures copy load and a real rendered 16:9 preview. It does not judge whether the idea is brilliant, truthful, or beautifully written.

**Useful answer:** keep one page, split at an honest boundary, edit before splitting, or make separate live/read versions.

### Deck Bones

Scripted Film & TV only: features and series. The quick ride uses bold structured choices and returns a practical page order. The deeper ride is optional and adds recipient shape, route, requirement status, boundaries, and deadline without introducing prose boxes.

**Useful answer:** the smallest deck that can create the next decision, plus what to leave out and what must happen before a deck would be honest.

### Next Move

Cross-industry. Starts with a lane and a recognisable situation. It answers the prior question: should you even work on a deck now?

**Useful answer:** one move, three concrete actions, one distraction to avoid, and—only when useful—a handoff into Slide Check or Deck Bones.

### Pitch Dictionary

Cross-industry and available from every tool. Search, browse by family, follow related concepts, or ask for the meaning in the current tool.

**Useful answer:** a plain definition, what the term is not, why it matters, and what to look at next. Closing the dictionary returns the user to the exact place they left.

## Shared memory without forced togetherness

The website carries only facts that help:

- a Deck Bones page can move into Slide Check with its page job intact;
- a Next Move answer can open the appropriate tool;
- checked slide advice can become a note in the plan;
- the dictionary can explain a term in the current tool’s context;
- answers stay in browser session storage and can be cleared.

No tool silently changes another tool’s answer. Handoffs are visible. The user remains the editor.

## What this does not do

- It does not write the deck.
- It does not understand arbitrary prose.
- It does not score creative quality.
- It does not predict whether somebody will buy, fund, produce, or love the project.
- It does not replace current submission requirements or a real conversation.
- It does not upload, analyse, or retain pitch material on a server.

That narrowness is not missing intelligence. It is product honesty.
